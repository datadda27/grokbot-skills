# Shelf-life table (US)

Version: Prepper v1.2.4 (2026-10-29)

Bundled because the live USDA FoodKeeper app blocks automated reads. Use the printed date on the package first. Use this table only when a package has no date, for home-canned food, or to set a rotate date. Two kinds of missing date: a date not read from a photo stays UNKNOWN (ask for a closer photo; never use this table for it); an undated package (no printed date at all) uses the low end of its range below and is marked estimated. Source for every food row: USDA FSIS, Shelf-Stable Food Safety, last updated Oct 24, 2024 (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/shelf-stable-food).

| Food | On the shelf | After opening |
|---|---|---|
| Low-acid canned goods (canned meat and poultry, stews, soups except tomato, pasta products, potatoes, corn, carrots, spinach, beans, beets, peas, pumpkin) | 2 to 5 years | 3 to 4 days refrigerated |
| High-acid canned goods (juices, tomatoes, fruit, pickles, sauerkraut, vinegar-based foods) | 12 to 18 months | 5 to 7 days refrigerated |
| Shelf-stable canned ham | 2 to 5 years | 3 to 4 days refrigerated |
| Home-canned foods | 12 months | 3 to 4 days refrigerated |
| Jerky, commercially packaged | 12 months | |
| Jerky, home-dried | 1 to 2 months | |
| Hard or dry sausage | 6 weeks in the pantry | 3 weeks refrigerated |
| Tuna and other seafood in retort pouches | 18 months | 3 to 4 days refrigerated |
| Meat or poultry in retort pouches | the maker's date on the package | 3 to 4 days refrigerated |
| Rice and dried pasta | 2 years | cooked: 3 to 4 days refrigerated |
| MREs | 60°F: 7 years; 70°F: 4.5 years; 80°F: 4 years; 90°F: 2.5 years; 100°F: 1.5 years; 120°F: 1 month | |

Other dates (non-food):
| Item | Rotate or check | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Water you bottled yourself | every 6 months | Ready.gov Water |
| Bleach for disinfecting water | use bottles stored under 1 year at room temperature | EPA |
| Smoke alarm 9-volt batteries | at least once a year; test monthly | USFA |
| Smoke alarm units | every 10 years | Ready.gov Home Fires |
| Batteries, medications, first aid items | the printed date | Ready.gov Kit (replace expired items) |

Storage rules from the same FSIS page: keep canned and shelf-stable food in a cool, dry place, best below 85°F; never above or beside the stove, under the sink, or in a damp garage or basement. Don't keep or buy bulging, leaking, rusted through, or deeply dented cans (a dent you can lay a finger into, or any dent on a seam). Dates on most shelf-stable food are for quality, not safety. The bot sets a rotate date at the low end of each range and calls it an estimate.
