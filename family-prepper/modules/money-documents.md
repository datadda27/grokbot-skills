# Module: Money and documents binder

Version: Prepper v1.2.4 (2026-10-29). Opt-in module, off by default. Loaded only when the owner turns it on.

Source: Ready.gov Financial Preparedness and its Emergency Financial First Aid Kit (EFFAK) (https://www.ready.gov/financial-preparedness).

## The binder checklist (yes / no / not sure, never contents)
From Ready.gov's list:
- Household identification: photo IDs, birth certificates, Social Security cards (needed to apply for FEMA assistance), military service records, pet ID tags.
- Financial and legal: housing payment records, insurance policies, sources of income, tax statements.
- Medical information: physician information, health insurance copies, immunization records, medication list (kept in the binder, never on the Page).
- Insurance: home or renters and other property coverage. Ready.gov: "Document and insure your property now."
- Household contacts: banks, insurance agents, health professionals, service providers, place of worship.
Store copies in a safety deposit box, on an external drive, or in the cloud so they are easy to reach in a disaster (Ready.gov), plus a waterproof bag of copies in the go-bag (Ready.gov Build a Kit: documents in a waterproof container or saved electronically).
Be careful sharing account or Social Security numbers; the government will not contact you through social media about payments (Ready.gov).

## Cash
Cash in small bills is tracked only in `private.json`, as yes or no. No amount anywhere: never asked for, stored, or shown on the Page, the printout, or a chat summary. The Page and the printable plan say nothing about cash. Small bills are easier during outages, when card readers may be down (Ready.gov Build a Kit lists cash).

## Benefits
Ready.gov suggests switching federal benefits to direct deposit or a Direct Express card, since a disaster can disrupt mail for days or weeks. The bot links it and says nothing about eligibility.

## Insurance checks (once a year, in the check-in)
- Flood: homeowner's policies do not cover flooding, and a new NFIP policy usually takes 30 days to start. (Ready.gov Floods, https://www.floodsmart.gov/)
- Earthquake: a standard homeowner's policy does not cover earthquake damage. (Ready.gov Earthquakes)
The bot never recommends a policy or insurer. Questions go to an insurance agent.
