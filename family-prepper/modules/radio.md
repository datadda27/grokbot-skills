# Module: Family radio track (FRS, GMRS, ham)

Version: Prepper v1.2.5 (2026-10-29). Opt-in add-on, off by default, never scored. The bot offers it only when the owner asks about radios or keeping in touch when phones are down. It never adds radio items to the score, the tiers, or the mainstream flow. Neutral: no brands or models.

Phones and texts stay the main plan. The out-of-area check-in and the "We're OK" text (SKILL.md, Family) come first; radios are a backup for short range.

## Step 1: FRS (no license)
- FRS radios need no license. The FCC says range is usually under half a mile on channels 8 to 14, the lower-power channels. (FCC FRS: https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/family-radio-service-frs)
- Good for keeping in touch around the house, the block, or a campsite.

## Step 2: GMRS (one license, no exam)
- GMRS is one $35 FCC license, good for 10 years, no exam, for someone 18 or older. It covers immediate family, including grandparents and in-laws (47 CFR 95.1705(c)(2), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-95/subpart-E/subject-group-ECFR6dc0a9b84f4b16e/section-95.1705). (FCC GMRS: https://www.fcc.gov/wireless/bureau-divisions/mobility-division/general-mobile-radio-service-gmrs)

## Step 3 (optional): ham Technician
Step 3 optional: ham Technician. Each person gets their own license: $35 to the FCC, plus an exam fee set by the exam team (ARRL sessions are $15 in 2026, $5 under 18, and ARRL reimburses the $35 FCC fee for new licensees under 18; other teams charge $0 to $15). The exam is 35 questions and you need 26 to pass, from the July 2026 to June 2030 pool. Study free at HamStudy.org (the paid app is optional, for offline study) and with the ARRL Tech Prep library. Find a session at hamstudy.org/sessions or the ARRL exam finder, which includes remote exams. GMRS mobiles and repeaters can run up to 50 W on the main channels. Realistic range (general estimates): handhelds about 1 to 2 miles, vehicle radios a few miles more. The FCC puts GMRS at 1 to 25 miles depending on radio, terrain and repeater. Out-of-state contacts are reached by phone or satellite text.

Links:

- HamStudy sessions: https://hamstudy.org/sessions
